O-1 Change of Status (extraordinary ability, filed from inside the U.S.).

Process for a non-citizen who is already in the United States in a valid
nonimmigrant status (for example F-1, H-1B, J-1 without a two-year home
residency requirement, or B-2) and wants to switch to O-1 temporary worker
status without leaving the country. A U.S. employer, or a U.S. agent acting
for the beneficiary, files Form I-129 with its O and P Classifications
Supplement, selecting O-1A (extraordinary ability in sciences, education,
business or athletics) or O-1B (extraordinary ability in the arts or
extraordinary achievement in motion pictures or television), and requests a
change of status in Part 2. The petition is supported by a peer-group or
labor-organization consultation, a contract or summary of terms, an itinerary
when there are several engagements, and evidence of sustained national or
international acclaim. A spouse or unmarried children under 21 who want O-3
dependent status file Form I-539 (with I-539A for each additional family
member). Premium processing (I-907) is optional.

Suitable when the user is physically in the U.S., has a U.S. employer or agent
willing to petition, and wants temporary work status based on extraordinary
ability. This process does not lead to a green card: an O-1 worker seeking
permanent residence through an I-140 (e.g. EB-1A) and I-485 uses Adjustment of
Status (aos) instead. It is also distinct from applying for an O-1 visa at a
U.S. consulate abroad, which uses the DS-160 after the I-129 is approved.
