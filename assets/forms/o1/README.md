O-1 extraordinary ability visa (temporary work status).

Process for a non-citizen with extraordinary ability who wants to work
temporarily in the United States in O-1 status. A U.S. employer, or a U.S.
agent acting for the beneficiary, files Form I-129 with its O and P
Classifications Supplement, selecting O-1A (extraordinary ability in the
sciences, education, business or athletics) or O-1B (extraordinary ability in
the arts or extraordinary achievement in motion pictures or television). The
petition is supported by a peer-group or labor-organization consultation, a
contract or summary of terms, an itinerary when there are several
engagements, and evidence of sustained national or international acclaim.
Premium processing (I-907) is optional.

How the rest is filed depends on where the beneficiary is:

- In the U.S. in a valid nonimmigrant status (for example F-1, H-1B, J-1
  without a two-year home residency requirement, or B-2): the I-129 requests a
  change of status in Part 2, so they switch to O-1 without leaving. A spouse
  or unmarried children under 21 who want O-3 dependent status file Form I-539
  (with I-539A for each additional family member).
- Abroad: the I-129 requests consular notification. After approval the
  beneficiary and any O-3 dependents apply for visas at a U.S. consulate with
  the DS-160, which is completed online with the State Department and is not
  covered.

Suitable when the user has a U.S. employer or agent willing to petition and
wants temporary work status based on extraordinary ability. This process does
not lead to a green card: an O-1 worker seeking permanent residence through an
I-140 (e.g. EB-1A) uses eb1 instead.
