Marriage-based green card (spouse of a U.S. citizen or permanent resident).

Process for a non-citizen who is married to a U.S. citizen or lawful
permanent resident and wants a green card through that marriage. The U.S.
spouse files Form I-130 (Petition for Alien Relative), with I-130A for the
foreign spouse, to prove the marriage is genuine, and Form I-864 (Affidavit
of Support) to show they can support the foreign spouse financially.

How the rest is filed depends on where the foreign spouse is:

- In the U.S. (adjustment of status): the foreign spouse files Form I-485
  together with the I-130 when married to a U.S. citizen, or once their
  priority date is current when married to a permanent resident. Optional
  forms filed alongside are I-765 (work permit) and I-131 (travel document),
  plus the I-693 medical exam.
- Abroad (consular processing): the I-130 is filed first, and after approval
  the National Visa Center collects the I-864 and civil documents. The
  immigrant visa application itself (DS-260) is completed online with the
  State Department and is not covered.

Also covers a fiancé(e) who entered on a K-1 visa and married the petitioner
within 90 days: they file the I-485, I-864, I-765 and I-131 without a new
I-130.

Suitable when the green card is based on a marriage that has already taken
place. An engaged couple with the foreign partner still abroad needs a fiancé
visa instead. Employment-based green cards through an I-140 (EB-1A, EB-1B,
EB-1C) use eb1, and temporary O-1 work status uses o1.
