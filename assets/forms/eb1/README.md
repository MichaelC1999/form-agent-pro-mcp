EB-1 employment-based green card (extraordinary ability, outstanding researcher or multinational manager).

Process for a non-citizen seeking lawful permanent resident (green card)
status in the first employment-based preference category. The immigrant
petition is Form I-140, in one of three subcategories:

- EB-1A, extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business or
  athletics. The beneficiary may self-petition; no employer or job offer is
  required. Evidence is sustained national or international acclaim, shown by
  a major one-time award or at least three of the ten regulatory criteria
  (awards, memberships, published material about the person, judging,
  original contributions, scholarly articles, exhibitions, leading or critical
  role, high salary, commercial success in the performing arts).
- EB-1B, outstanding professor or researcher. Requires a permanent research
  or tenure-track job offer from a U.S. university or qualifying employer and
  at least three years of research or teaching experience.
- EB-1C, multinational manager or executive. Requires a U.S. employer that
  has employed the beneficiary abroad for at least one of the last three years
  in a managerial or executive role at a related (parent, branch, subsidiary
  or affiliate) entity.

Premium processing (I-907) is optional for the I-140. When the beneficiary is
physically in the U.S. and their priority date is current for their country of
chargeability, they may file Form I-485 together with the I-140 (concurrent
filing), with I-765 work authorization, I-131 travel document and I-693
medical exam. EB-1B and EB-1C adjustment applicants also file I-485
Supplement J to confirm the job offer; EB-1A self-petitioners do not. When the
priority date is not current (often the case for applicants born in India or
China), only the I-140 is filed now and the I-485 comes later. A spouse and
unmarried children under 21 in the U.S. each file their own I-485 as
derivatives.

Suitable when the user wants permanent residence based on extraordinary
ability, research achievement or a multinational managerial role. It is
distinct from the O-1 visa (o1), which uses similar evidence but grants
temporary work status only, and from a marriage-based green card (marriage). A
beneficiary abroad files only the I-140 here; the later immigrant visa
application (DS-260) is completed online with the State Department and is not
covered.
